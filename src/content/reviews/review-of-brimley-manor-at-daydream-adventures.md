Daydream has become well known for their detailed custom designed props and artist created decor. Their sets are quite beautiful with a lot of attention to detail. This room is a near perfect 360 degrees of immersion with walls, floors, lighting, and audio cues all well thought out throughout. The only finishing touch that's missing is the ceilings but that's a detail that isn't going to affect the experience much in this dark spooky home. We also found the "van" to be an okay effort but a bit oversimplified.

Our game master was fantastically warm and welcoming and did well explaining elements of the game once we were complete. We needed nudges a couple of times in the game as found there were times where you sort of wonder "what's next" and think a couple of stronger nudges in the recorded audio or video elements would be helpful. There's also no way to ask a ghost to repeat themselves if you miss something.

Most puzzles are magical (electronic) with just a couple of traditional locks. They incorporated some unique puzzle elements that we really enjoyed but found they leaned a bit too much into gamification in some places. This may be great for younger players but more experienced escape room enthusiasts may find them out of place. We felt that sometimes Daydream can focus too much on the beauty of a room and the magic of electronics and miss out on the core of what escape room enthusiasts are looking for - challenging head scratcher puzzles that make you feel like you didn't just do something, you solved something.

This room was a medium hard room though we're torn on whether some concepts were too abstract. There's a lot to do in this room but the puzzles follow the story and so they're mostly linear. The space is large enough to accommodate 6 to 8 players but we think that four players is probably the sweet spot.

This room is a lot of fun and really made and we'd do it again! We think you should give it a shot.
