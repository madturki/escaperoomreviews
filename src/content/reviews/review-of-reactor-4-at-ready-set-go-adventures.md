_It’s your very first day as a new hire at the Mid-West Nuclear Power Plant. You’re assigned to Reactor 4 – It’s old, dark, and grungy, but, it has an immaculate safety reputation… Until today. Something has gone horribly wrong! Reactor 4 has gone into a Code-Red! Alarms are roaring, bells are buzzing and smoke is filling the plant! Intense to say the least. It’s up to you to fix all 10 systems and stabilize the nuclear core before it’s too late! Do you have what it takes?_

We have not yet completed a review of Reactor 4 at Ready Set Go Adventures in Mississauga. We hope to experience this room soon.

## What others are saying

Reactor 4 gets a warm reception from escape room fans. Like all Ready Set Go games, it's point-based: your team earns points by fixing the plant's systems, and the story keeps moving even if you fall behind. It isn't meant to be scary, and players on Morty and the We Try To Escape blog generally rank it among the company's stronger rooms.

The set is the big draw. We Try To Escape praised the detailed, well-kept decor and the clever use of different levels, and several Morty reviewers singled out the final room as a standout. Players describe the puzzles as challenging, multi-step and tied closely to the nuclear theme, with almost everything run by automated tech instead of locks. Because the room is non-linear and many puzzles need two people, reviewers say nobody ends up standing around, which makes it a good pick for bigger groups. Staff get regular compliments too.

The common complaints are about sensory overload and memory. Some players found the constant alarms overwhelming, especially during puzzles that rely on listening, and a few said the room leans heavily on memorization. One pair felt a puzzle needed a stretch of logic too far and suggested going with at least three people. That same pair found it confusing when the game moved them along automatically. People who have played other Ready Set Go rooms noticed familiar puzzle formats, and one wished the opening area had the same polish as the finale.
