Witness is an attempt at a Black Mirror style investigation of a crime by going into the memories of the deceased. You start off at the police station speaking to a police officer before returning to the victim's home to examine clues and figure out what really happened.

I will point out that the location did not have a washroom available which we *(and Toronto bylaw)* think is important when a group is in your location for a one hour experience.

The physical police station space feels like something they started to design as a futuristic police station and decided to hastily attach LED strips around the wall. The police officer is a TV with a static picture of a police officer playing audio with subtitles. Occasionally you have to enter in an answer or click a button on a keyboard and mouse balanced on a stool (a small board attached at working height to the wall, below the TV would be much better). Out of your one hour experience, at least 15 minutes are spent waiting and listening to the dialogue on this TV.

Once you move into the home you're greeted with a wall of "not part of the game" objects on one wall, and a wall of "objects not part of the game" on the other side. Only two items are really used in this space and three items that you find. Except that two out of three locations are lit up in this dark space and the other is impossible to open without some sharp fingernails.

There is another small space which feels unfinished and contains one puzzle (a repeated concept from earlier in the game). And then finally you can move into the "bonus area" where you're greeted with seven fairly random puzzles that mostly don't relate to the original theme and are like a time warp into a first-generation escape room. Most items are out of play. Some in play items seemed to be painters tape with ballpoint pen on them.

There are a lot of audio and lighting accents in this room but the incomplete feeling of the spaces and the passive nature of the experience (way too much sitting and listening to the story) made us want to just stop playing. This was probably only the third time out of nearly 160 rooms that we were uninterested in finishing.

With so many other great locations very close by, we wouldn't do this room again.
