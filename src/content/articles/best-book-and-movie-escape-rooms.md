There's something special about stepping into a world you already love. Book and movie themed rooms let you walk the deck of Captain Hook's ship, tumble down the rabbit hole, or wander through a cartoon house you've watched on TV for decades. The challenge for the builders is that everyone already knows what these places should look like, and the best rooms on this list nail it with great sets, nods for fans, and puzzles you can enjoy even if you've never read the book.

This list covers every book and movie room we've played, wherever we've played it, from Kingston to Quebec City. It's ranked by our review scores and only includes rooms that are still open.

![Neverland: Heist on the High Seas at Improbable Escapes](/images/2021/10/neverland-heist-on-the-high-seas-1.jpg)

## 1. [Neverland: Heist on the High Seas](/review-of-neverland-heist-on-the-high-seas/) (Kingston)

**Our score: 9.3/10** · Improbable Escapes

Captain Hook has locked you in the brig of The Jolly Roger, and you have 60 minutes to take the ship and set a course for Neverland. This room redefined the word "immersive" for us, with near-perfect audio, lighting, and physical props, plus a wide variety of tricky puzzles that never felt repetitive. Our only nitpicks were a slight overuse of technology in one puzzle and a tweak we'd make to the exit door.

## 2. [Walden’s Wizarding Shop](/review-of-waldens-wizarding-shop-at-looking-glass-adventures/) (Toronto)

**Our score: 9/10** · Looking Glass Adventures

Who's minding Walden's shop? Grab your wand and help him stop the evil forces that lurk inside. There are some nods to your favourite books about wizards, but we're not deeply into those books and it didn't stop us from enjoying the well-made puzzles one bit. It took us the longest of all their rooms and felt like the best value, with magical cues guiding you along.

## 3. [Madder Tea Party](/review-of-madder-tea-party-at-ichabods-escape/) (London)

**Our score: 8.5/10** · Ichabod's

Save Alice and your team from the Queen of Hearts in this leap down the rabbit hole. The room is filled with beautiful art, clever props, and new styles of puzzles, and there isn't a single padlock or combination lock in the place. One puzzle near the end has two possible answers but only one works, so if you get stuck, try the other way. There's also some noise from adjacent rooms.

[Book it Now](https://ichabodsescape.com/book-now/)

## 4. [Trouble in Springfield](/review-of-trouble-in-springfield-at-hint-hunt/) (Mississauga)

**Our score: 8.1/10** · Hint Hunt

Springfield's residents have vanished and the appliances are going haywire. Can you prevent the meltdown? Hint Hunt managed to make you feel like you're on the set of a show many of us grew up with, complete with bright colours and sharp edges, and the non-linear puzzles all fit the theme. We did receive some items earlier than we needed them and ran into a big red herring. Bring 3 to 5 players to explore it faster.

## 5. [Chamber of Serpents](/review-of-chamber-of-serpents-at-e-exit/) (Toronto)

**Our score: 8/10** · E-Exit

A misfired spell in the Hogwarts library leads you to a clue about the hidden chambers of Salazar Slytherin. The distinct, well-lit areas look good, and the logical, linear puzzles follow the story closely. It's a great choice for newer groups or families with kids. The story delivery lacked a bit of enthusiasm and the ending was a little weird, and more than 4 players may leave some people standing around.

## 6. [Au Pays Des Merveilles](/review-of-au-pays-des-merveilles-at-a-double-tour/) (Quebec City)

**Our score: 7.8/10** · A Double Tour

A classic Alice in Wonderland room that you can play entirely in English or French. The staff stay in character from the introduction to the end, the hint system is fantastic, and clever construction makes the space feel like a smaller world. It can be an acrobatic adventure if you're a bit larger or playing as a duo, so we recommend 3 or 4 players. We'd love to see a more immersive wrap-up at the end.

## 7. [Seven Dwarfs: Mining Mission](/review-of-seven-dwarfs-mining-mission-at-improbable-escapes/) (Kingston)

**Our score: 7.8/10** · Improbable Escapes

Snow White needs you to destroy the poison apple by casting it into the centre of the earth. It's one of the most immersive rooms we've ever done, and it truly feels like you're in a cottage and then in a dwarf mine, with plenty of technology balanced by dark, rugged puzzles. One item came down to interpretation, and seeing a colour differently than the designers cost us a lot of time. It's a medium-hard room.

## 8. [The Thompsons](/review-of-the-thompsons-at-next-level/) (Hamilton)

**Our score: 7.8/10** · Next Level

Your neighbours have gone into witness protection as The Thompsons, and the FBI still can't find the culprit. Clearly modelled after The Simpsons, this cartoon world is very immersive and makes you feel like you've stepped inside one of your favourite TV shows. The puzzles and the way you get around the room are unique, though one puzzle was a bit confusing. If you don't like hints, tell the host clearly.

## 9. [Wonderland](/review-of-wonderland-at-escape-manor/) (Ottawa)

**Our score: 7.8/10** · Escape Manor

You've eaten crumpets that made you gigantic, and the only cure is the Queen of Hearts' tarts. This fun, youthful room is a nice break from the usual pirates, mummies, and horror, with great props, good puzzles, and a very clear story. The tiny furniture can feel a bit like a toy store, and some exposed technical elements hurt the immersion. It's good for all ages and fits 4 to 6 people easily.
