These are the workbenches: generators for the paper, sample puzzles worth studying, and the practice sets new designers use to get faster.

## Making the paper

Breakout EDU's [puzzle resources](https://www.breakoutedu.com/puzzleresources) and [Escape Team](https://www.escape-team.com/) both collect generators for tickets, receipts, codes, and maps. Individual tools that keep coming up:

- [Fake tickets](http://www.faketicketgenerator.com/)
- [Receipts](https://makereceipt.com/create-receipt.php)
- [Text messages](https://ifaketextmessage.com/)
- [Headlines](https://breakyourownnews.com/)
- [Fantasy maps](https://azgaar.github.io/Fantasy-Map-Generator/) and [treasure maps](https://treasuremaps.kadsoftwareusa.com/)

A web search for "fake" plus the object you need still turns up good one-off generators.

For words and codes: [Nutrimatic](https://nutrimatic.org/) and [Qat](https://www.quinapalus.com/qat.html) search with constraints a normal dictionary cannot. [Tilt-to-read text](https://codepen.io/ninivert/full/JEPzxO) is a small visual trick. Constructed Adventures has short videos on [folding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zaNBSymOSIU), [word searches](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hxT2e1SNwSo), [hiding a message in playing cards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QVfmpNuXDYQ), and a [rubber-band code](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=prRYljNmMXg).

Escape Room Tips keeps an [assortment of puzzle mechanics](https://escaperoomtips.com/design/escape-room-puzzle-ideas).

## Events and hunts worth studying

Rita Orlov designed [a wedding hunt](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/puzzlecrafting-a-wedding-hunt). Room Escape Artist wrote up [another wedding puzzle](https://roomescapeartist.com/2016/06/19/spira-wedding-puzzle/).

[Puzzled Pint](http://www.puzzledpint.com/) posts a monthly set meant to be solved with friends over a drink. [DASH](http://playdash.org/) is an annual city-wide puzzle event, and the puzzles go online after each one. Two sample mechanics from Puzzled Pint show the range: a [process puzzle aimed at fun](https://puzzledpint.com/archive-files/4615/6596/5407/01_Aug2019_Bork_Bork_Bork.pdf), and a [mix of insight, trivia, and process](https://puzzledpint.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/MainSet_ChristmasInJuly1.pdf). Trivia only works when you know who is playing.

Puzzle hunts are usually too hard for a one-hour room. The shared ideas are catalogued at [Puzzles Wiki](https://www.puzzles.wiki/wiki/Main_Page) and on [Vero's puzzle-hunt notes](https://blog.vero.site/post/puzzlehunts). The [2017 MIT Mystery Hunt documentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J8dqmk4YCQk) is worth watching with that warning. Around the 31-minute mark, Chromesthesia is the example of a puzzle that does not belong in an escape room. Curtis C. Chen [breaks down puzzles from The Game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QGXzqc1v5hI), the classic hunt.

The design argument for borrowing from hunts, carefully, is on the [puzzle design](/escape-room-puzzle-design/) page.

## Practice for new puzzlers

[Enigma Emporium](https://www.facebook.com/enigmaemporium) posts entry-level puzzles. [Game Detective Academy](https://gamedetectives.net/academy/) is a set of interactive courses in solving. Both are useful if you are training staff, or yourself, before you ask players to do the same thing.
