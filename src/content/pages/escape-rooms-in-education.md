An educational room still has to work as a game. These are the books, classroom writeups, and course outlines that treat both halves seriously.

## Books and arguments

Julia Morris's [*Escape Rooms in Education*](https://www.amazon.com/Escape-Rooms-Education-Practical-Guide-ebook/dp/B08LHK2CRQ) is a practical guide, with general design advice and worked examples of physical and digital puzzles. Room Escape Artist's [review](https://roomescapeartist.com/2021/06/09/escape-rooms-education-practical-guide-julia-morris-book-review/) is a useful second pass.

Scott Nicholson and Liz Cable's [*Unlocking the Potential of Puzzle-based Learning*](https://us.corwin.com/en-us/nam/unlocking-the-potential-of-puzzle-based-learning/book271877) is the broader case for puzzles as a way to teach. Shauna Pollock's [list of elements, challenges, and reasons](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1plNYUShXL9ms8lM6V8jBmXIB65MR5cm8nc5LyY9mUKM/edit) is the teacher's checklist. Brian Mayer and Liesl Toates in School Library Journal on [bringing escape rooms into education](https://www.slj.com/?detailStory=all-about-escape-rooms). The Atlantic's [rise of educational escape rooms](https://www.theatlantic.com/education/archive/2016/07/the-rise-of-educational-escape-rooms/493316/) is the overview from outside the field.

## Rooms that were built to teach

The University of Arizona added an [escape room to a medical school curriculum](https://phoenixmed.arizona.edu/about/news/medical-school-adds-escape-room-curriculum). A pharmacology class used a tabletop escape and wrote up what transferred. The Findings and Lessons Learned sections of [that paper](https://www.sciedu.ca/journal/index.php/jnep/article/viewFile/12297/7833) are the part worth stealing, whether or not you teach medicine.

[*A Meteor Crashed Into Our School*](http://thatguywiththepuzzles.com/dlfiles/AMeteorCrashedIntoOurSchool.pdf) is an immersive week of cross-curricular work, from NSTA's *Science & Children*. Fire Engineering describes [escape-style scenarios for firefighter training](https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighter-training/escape-room-fire-training).

Stanford's d.school used an escape room to [measure deeper learning](https://dschool.stanford.edu/stories/the-deeper-learning-escape-room), with a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sLvNLMPjt64) and the [design files](https://drive.google.com/drive/u/0/folders/1q330BYxhnh-9Puww1EIncpYzfu6yQfBD).

## Courses

Peter Kallok and Ira Fay taught escape room design at Hampshire College. The [course page](http://irafay.com/classes/CS281/) and a [No Proscenium interview](https://noproscenium.com/escape-room-course-brings-together-game-design-and-theatre-in-amherst-q-a-d396bb09e1ad) describe a class that sat between game design and theatre. Ira Fay's [reading list](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1e8Yf8_HSTDjimgD0jVMx6aDZYEYYPrlRoY1PXq9Yzsk), linked from the [communities](/escape-room-design-communities/) page, is the wider shelf of game-design reading.

## Research

One paper treats escape rooms as a [social laboratory for team communication](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-13929-0). It is the research piece in this collection, rather than a how-to.
