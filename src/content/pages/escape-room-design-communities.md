Design gets better when you can ask someone. These are the communities and the other lists people pass around, plus a few neighbouring fields that keep turning out to be relevant.

## Where people talk

- [Escape Room Discord](https://discord.gg/BtWQAFtRE4)
- [Constructed Adventures](https://www.reddit.com/r/Constructedadventures/) on Reddit, and their [Discord](https://discord.gg/s4ZjDAujBD)
- The [Escape Room Props and Tech](https://www.facebook.com/groups/493838418258543) group on Facebook

## Other lists

Dan Egnor's [FAQ](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1d8NQhT1XEffpLYkcvhyEgF8bpsMEWEEgERtEd6_ea7g/edit#heading=h.4mim6q7xg2xa) and Ira Fay's [reading list](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1e8Yf8_HSTDjimgD0jVMx6aDZYEYYPrlRoY1PXq9Yzsk) are the two documents people keep beside this one. [The Really Fun Spectrum](https://brettkuehner.com/tgwtp/The%20Really%20Fun%20Spectrum%20v1.0%20-%20Really%20Fun%20%282020-08-04%209_04_54%20PM%29.html) is a visual taxonomy of kinds of games. The copy here is the archived page.

For blogs that review and write about rooms, rather than design communities, see [Global Escape Room Blogs](/global-escape-room-blogs/).

## Nearby fields

C. Thi Nguyen's paper on [agency in games](https://www.pgrim.org/philosophersannual/39articles/nguyen-games.pdf) is philosophy, and it is useful when you are deciding what the player is allowed to do. Sam Kabo Ashwell's [patterns for choice](https://heterogenoustasks.wordpress.com/2015/01/26/standard-patterns-in-choice-based-games/) is the same question drawn as a diagram. It is also linked from the [story](/escape-room-story-and-immersion/) page, because that is where the choice usually shows up.
