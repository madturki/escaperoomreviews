Principles do not book the build. These are the writeups of people starting from a blank page, mapping the hour, and leaving the design where someone else can read it.

## Starting

[*Do(n't) Panic!*](https://brettkuehner.com/tgwtp/Do%28n't%29%20Panic%20-%20Escape%20Room%20Manual%20Final.pdf) is a step-by-step guide written around a public-library room. It is especially useful if the game has to live in a library, a museum, or another space you do not control. The original Drive file now asks for permission. This PDF is the copy that still opens.

Rita Orlov's [where to begin](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/creating-experiences-where-to-begin) is the shorter starting question. Her designer diaries on [sources of inspiration](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/designer-diary-1) and [more of that diary](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/designer-diary-8) show the early mess before a game has a shape.

EscapeFront's [concept-design worksheet](http://thatguywiththepuzzles.com/dlfiles/PlanninganEscapeRoom_ConceptDesign-EscapeFront.html) is a planning page you can steal. Lock Paper Scissors on [crafting a first room](https://lockpaperscissors.co/craft-1st-escape-room) is aimed at someone who has not built one yet.

## Mapping and testing ideas quickly

Strange Bird's [mapping](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/mapping/) shows how to draw the flow, the events, and the puzzles, and names tools that help. An [escape jam](https://www.jebhavensgames.com/blog/the-first-ever-escape-jam-design-workshop) is one way to produce a low-tech game fast enough to test.

## Designs you can read

Tori Lynn Smith's [No Worries](http://torilynnsmith.com/no-worries) is a short experience built on environmental storytelling, and on emotions rooms do not usually chase. She posted the design documents.

Students in the University of Washington Interactive Media Design program made The Fate of the SS Fortuna. The [trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eAg0izkYKxg) is the public record of that build.

Scientiffic's [walkthrough of a popup room for TechX](https://scientiffic.medium.com/designing-a-pop-up-room-escape-f3dee43b4e68) is a full pass over a temporary build.

Portable games are the same process at a smaller scale. One [homemade portable escape](https://imgur.com/a/B2wCwRt) is photographed end to end, and Chris Ramsay [plays one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eXY017IWVjE).
