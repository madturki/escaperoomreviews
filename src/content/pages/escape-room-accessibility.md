A room that only works for one kind of body is a smaller room. These are the references for the legal floor and for the design choices above it.

EscapeFront's [Escape Rooms and the ADA](http://thatguywiththepuzzles.com/dlfiles/EscapeRoomsandTheADA-EscapeFront.html) covers legal requirements in the United States and practical ways to adapt a game.

Chris Fairfield's [colorblind-friendly game design](https://chrisfairfield.com/unlocking-colorblind-friendly-game-design/) is specific, with examples of what fails and what to do instead. Rita Orlov's designer diary on [accessibility in puzzle design](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/designer-diary-5) is the puzzle-maker's version.

[The Owl Job](http://www.theowljob.com/) is a detailed account of designing a room for sightless play. The examples apply to rooms that are not built for that audience, and the page includes a list of further resources.

Two companies publish the changes they actually offer. [Escape Key](https://www.escape-key.co.uk/accessibility) and [Random Rooms](https://www.random-rooms.co.uk/access-statement.html) are useful as models of what to write down, not only as rooms to visit.
