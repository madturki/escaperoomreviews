A puzzle can be fair and still be dull. These are the pieces that separate a clever mechanism from a puzzle people are glad they solved.

## What a good puzzle is

Errol Elumir's slide deck, [The Art of Escape Room Puzzle Design](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1OtnXhkJcThRbgBu3B0DKCN3A05GB6hnd2vc_sn1p5ns), is the compact version of his thinking. [Aha puzzles versus process puzzles](https://thecodex.ca/puzzles-aha-vs-process/) is the distinction worth learning first: a sudden insight and a stretch of work are different pleasures. His [13 rules](https://thecodex.ca/13-rules-for-escape-room-puzzle-design/) are the checklist that follows.

Two short talks cover the same ground for people who would rather watch it: [five things new designers should know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TUvBd_4OSc), and [designing puzzles players enjoy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mKC09H50TMU).

Rita Orlov's [what makes a good puzzle](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/what-makes-a-good-puzzle) and [balancing difficulty](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/balancing-difficulty) are the plain-language pair. The Wild Optimists, who made Escape Room in a Box, put a lot of practical rules into [one talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOg1YLFw55E). Brian Hamrick's [puzzle construction](http://brianhamrick.com/blog/puzzle-construction) is the general-principles version.

Elyot Grant's three-part series is a full course if you want one sitting: [part one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oCHciE9CYfA), [part two](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iUi2vMZajco), [part three](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zsbfkMuaUxs).

## Particular kinds of puzzle

Escape The Review on [making a search feel fair](https://escapethereview.co.uk/how-to-make-searching-fun/). Rita Orlov walks through building one puzzle in public in [a symbol combination cipher](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/making-a-puzzle-symbol-combination-cipher). Room Escape Artist on [priming](https://roomescapeartist.com/2020/10/21/priming-puzzles-escape-rooms/), which is how you teach a rule before you ask players to use it.

Constructed Adventures has a pile of short videos on [simple mechanisms](https://www.youtube.com/@ConstructedAdventures/videos). Liz Cable talks about [turning a book into a room](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ozyIBfQf-ws). Cully Long's [*How to Puzzle Cache*](https://www.amazon.com/How-Puzzle-Cache-Cully-Long/dp/131275382X) is about geocaching puzzles, and a lot of the construction advice moves straight across.

The [logic puzzle taxonomy](http://ludeme.eu/outputs/logic_puzzle_taxonomy.pdf) is a map of logic-puzzle types. Rod Kimball keeps a living list of [tools for puzzle makers](https://www.enigami.fun/puzzle-makers-resources). Patrick Traynor's [idea strategies](https://cwpat.me/misc/puzzle-level-idea-strategies/) are prompts for when the page is blank. Mairi on [getting puzzle-design work](https://www.mairispaceship.com/design-a-puzzle-game) is a career note with a lot of design links attached.

## What puzzle hunts can teach a room

Puzzle hunts are usually too hard to drop into a 60-minute room. The design habits travel. Alex Rosenthal's TED talk is the friendly introduction to [puzzle hunts](https://www.ted.com/talks/alex_rosenthal_the_joyful_perplexing_world_of_puzzle_hunts?language=en). David Morgan-Mar writes about [designing hunt puzzles](https://irregularwebcomic.net/3244.html). Andrew Plotkin on [puzzle structure and the meta](http://gameshelf.jmac.org/2011/01/puzzle-structure-in-2015/). Room Escape Artist's essay on [what rooms can learn from hunts](https://roomescapeartist.com/2025/08/28/what-escape-rooms-can-learn-from-puzzle-hunts/) is the piece written for this audience.

Worked examples, with the difficulty warning attached, are on the [puzzle tools](/escape-room-puzzle-tools/) page.

## Playtesting

Rita Orlov's testing notes come in three parts: the [process](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/puzzle-testing-process), then [playtesting, part one](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/playtesting-puzzle-games-pt-1) and [part two](https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/playtesting-puzzle-games-pt-2).

The Society of Curiosities published their [escape room playtest process](https://blog.societyofcuriosities.com/2025/05/07/our-escape-room-playtest-process/). Board-game testing transfers more than people expect: [Playtest Like a Boss](https://boardgamedesignlab.com/playtest-like-a-boss/) and the BoardGameGeek writeup of [playtesting lessons from UX research](https://boardgamegeek.com/blog/16479/blogpost/175176/playtesting-lessons-from-ux-research).

Story needs its own tests. That argument is on the [story and immersion](/escape-room-story-and-immersion/) page.
